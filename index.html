build —

Method. 4,000 simulated eval attempts per recalc (fixed seed, so results are stable); every passing sim continues onto a funded account until it busts or the funded horizon runs out, with any remaining profit withdrawn at the end. Wins pay +R × risk, losses −1 × risk, drawn independently — real losing streaks cluster harder than that, so treat these as an optimistic ceiling. Your weekly trade count is spread across all 7 days; daily loss is checked against each day's starting balance, and max drawdown is a static floor from the initial balance.

Rules and fees are each firm's own published figures — breakoutprop.com/pricing (31 Aug 2026) and propr.xyz/rules (1 Sep 2026) — for 1-step plans only. Propr's Classic 2-Step uses a trailing drawdown across two stages and is deliberately not modelled here. Trading costs are Breakout's flat 0.04% per side with 0.033%/day financing, and for Propr the Hyperliquid rates it passes through at cost, where funding is peer-to-peer and a short can be paid it. Cross-firm comparisons use the 80/20 split both firms sell. Verify pricing before buying. Your inputs and journal are saved in this browser only. Not financial advice.

        Sizing strategy

        Each trade is sized so that losing it costs a fixed slice of today's allowance — commissions and financing included — so the whole day's worth of stops still lands inside the limit, with the rest held back to absorb a bad fill.

        The trade

        For TradingView replay and other backtesting. The trade is stamped with the time you set rather than now, and it is never marked against the live market — so a stop or target cannot fire off a price that has not happened yet in your replay. Type the replay price into the position yourself as you step the bars forward.
        The date and time on the replay chart, not the clock on the wall. Everything that counts by day — the daily loss limit, the trades-per-day budget — uses this.

        Max hold is a commitment, not a guess: the position is sized to afford financing for that long, so a shorter trade simply costs less. Overstay it and your real risk creeps past budget.
